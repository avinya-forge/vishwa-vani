## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Framework

Vishwa-Vani operates under a strict Open Heritage & Public Domain mandate. Every root Sanskrit verse, Devanagari text, Roman IAST transliteration, word-by-word grammatical gloss, and traditional commentary hosted in our Normalized Vedic Fragment (NVF) repository is verified to be in the **Public Domain** worldwide.

### Legal Baseline (Berne Convention & US/UK/Indian Copyright Law):
1. **Root Sanskrit Texts:** Ancient foundational scriptures composed between 1500 BCE and 500 CE. Under Indian Copyright Act (1957) and US Title 17, ancient religious and classical texts have no copyright owner and exist in the universal public domain.
2. **Classical Commentaries:** Composed by classical sages and āchāryas (Adi Shankaracharya, Sant Dnyaneshwar, Ramanujacharya, Madhvacharya, Sayanacharya, Sridhara Swami, Nilakantha Chaturdhara, Govindaraja) between the 8th and 17th centuries CE. All authorial rights expired centuries ago.
3. **Scholarly Translations:** We strictly utilize editions and translations published prior to 1928, or works whose authors passed away over 70 years ago (e.g., K.M. Ganguli 1896, R.T.H. Griffith 1899, H.H. Wilson 1840, M.N. Dutt 1908, W.D. Whitney 1905, Georg Bühler 1886, Swami Vivekananda 1896, Swami Dayananda Saraswati 1880).
4. **Zero Proprietary Infringement:** No copyrighted modern translations (such as modern commercial press translations with active proprietary claims) are copied or reproduced without verified open-source licensing.

## 3. Strict Anti-Hallucination & Provenance Audit Protocol

To ensure 100% authenticity and scholarly fidelity across Vishwa-Vani:

1. **Physical Text Cross-Referencing:** Every Sanskrit root verse in `data/silver/` and `lib/daily-shloka.ts` is cross-verified against at least two historical printed editions (e.g., Nirnaya Sagar Press, Chaukhamba, or Max Müller Critical Edition).
2. **Metrical Anustubh / Vedic Chhandas Integrity:** Sanskrit verses maintain exact classical metrical constraints (8-syllable pādas for Anuṣṭubh, 11-syllable for Triṣṭubh, 12-syllable for Jagatī, 8-8-8 for Gāyatrī). No AI-generated or modified shlokas are permitted.
3. **Attributed Lineage Commentaries:** Every scholar commentary box clearly attributes the sage's historical name, era, and spiritual sampradāya/philosophical lineage (e.g., *Advaita Vedānta*, *Viśiṣṭādvaita*, *Warkari Bhakti*, *Dvaita*, *Practical Vedānta*).
4. **Dynamic Synchronized Counting:** All shloka, chapter, and book counts in the UI are dynamically derived at runtime from the verified database and scripture metadata—preventing fabricated or repeated counts.
