# Future Public Domain Commentaries for Vishwa-Vani

Based on research for authentic, copyright-free (Public Domain) commentaries and translations that support regional Indian languages (e.g., Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi):

## 1. Srimad Bhagavadgita Rahasya by Bal Gangadhar Tilak
- **Language**: Marathi (Original), extensively translated into Hindi, Gujarati, and English.
- **Publication Year**: 1915
- **Copyright Status**: Public Domain (Author died in 1920).
- **Significance**: A monumental commentary that emphasizes *Karma Yoga* (the path of selfless action). Highly authentic and influential in the Indian independence movement.

## 2. Bhagavad Gita by Subramania Bharati
- **Language**: Tamil
- **Publication Year**: ~1912
- **Copyright Status**: Public Domain (Author died in 1921. His works were nationalized and placed into the public domain by the Madras Government in 1949).
- **Significance**: An authentic, poetic, and highly revered translation of the Gita in Tamil by one of the greatest modern Tamil literary figures.

## 3. Srimad Bhagavad Gita by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay
- **Language**: Bengali
- **Publication Year**: ~1902 (Posthumously)
- **Copyright Status**: Public Domain (Author died in 1894).
- **Significance**: A critical and philosophical Bengali commentary by the legendary author of Vande Mataram. It significantly shaped modern Bengali Hindu philosophical thought.

## 4. Sri Ramanuja Gita Bhashya (Translated by A. Govindacharya)
- **Language**: English/Sanskrit (Represents the Tamil/Vishishtadvaita Sri Vaishnava tradition)
- **Publication Year**: 1898
- **Copyright Status**: Public Domain.
- **Significance**: Brings the foundational Visistadvaita commentary of Ramanujacharya into an accessible PD English translation, complementing Shankara's Advaita bhashya.

## 5. The Bhagavad Gita by Swami Swarupananda
- **Language**: English with Sanskrit text (Ramakrishna Math / Bengali tradition context)
- **Publication Year**: 1909
- **Copyright Status**: Public Domain.
- **Significance**: Deeply influenced by the Bengali intellectual renaissance and Swami Vivekananda's teachings, it offers a highly accessible Advaitic commentary.
